Special Moves ♟️

Chess has some unusual moves that every beginner should know! Let's discover three special rules.

Your Chessboard

This is a decorative board for learning. The special moves are explained below!

1. Castling

- Castling is a special move involving your King and one of your Rooks.

- It is the only chess move where two pieces move during the same turn.

- The King moves two squares toward the Rook.

- The Rook moves to the square the King crossed over.

- Castling helps protect your King and can bring your Rook into the game.

Castling is allowed only when:

- Your King has never moved before.

- The Rook used for castling has never moved before.

- All squares between the King and that Rook are empty.

- Your King is not currently in check.

- The King does not pass through or land on a square attacked by an opponent's piece.

- The King and Rook must be on their original squares for that side of the board.

Remember!

You cannot castle out of check, through check, or into check!

2. Pawn Promotion

- Pawns usually move forward one square at a time.

- But what happens when a Pawn reaches the very last rank on the opposite side of the board?

- It gets promoted!

- You must replace the Pawn with a Queen, Rook, Bishop, or Knight of the same colour.

- Most players choose a Queen because it is the most powerful piece.

- However, choosing a Knight can sometimes be more useful because of its unusual L-shaped movement.

- You can promote to a piece even if that piece has already been captured earlier in the game.

- A Pawn cannot promote to a King.

Remember!

Your Pawn does not have to become a Queen. Choose whichever piece helps your position!

3. En Passant

- En passant is a special Pawn capture.

- The name is French and means "in passing".

- Imagine your opponent moves a Pawn forward two squares from its starting position.

- If that Pawn lands beside your Pawn, your Pawn may be able to capture it as though it had moved forward only one square.

- Your Pawn moves diagonally to the square the opposing Pawn passed over.

- The opposing Pawn is then removed from the board.

Important rules:

- Your Pawn must be on the correct rank to make the capture.

- The opposing Pawn must have just moved two squares from its starting square and landed beside your Pawn.

- You must make the en passant capture immediately on your next move. If you make another move first, the opportunity is gone.

Remember!

En passant is only possible immediately after your opponent's Pawn makes that two-square move.

Quick Recap!

- Castling: Move your King and Rook together under special conditions.

- Pawn Promotion: Turn a Pawn that reaches the opposite end of the board into a Queen, Rook, Bishop, or Knight.

- En Passant: Make a special Pawn capture immediately after an opposing Pawn moves two squares and lands beside yours.
